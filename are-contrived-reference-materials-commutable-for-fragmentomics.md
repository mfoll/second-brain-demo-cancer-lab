# Are Contrived Reference Materials Commutable For Fragmentomics?

## Why It Matters

If no available contrived material behaves like patient plasma for fragment-length and end-motif features, fragmentomics assays can never be calibrated or quality-controlled with reference materials, only with plasma. That changes how Halo, and any future external quality scheme, can treat our assay.

## Evidence So Far

- Sheared genomic DNA: clearly not commutable. Corvane scored it as high-risk regardless of mutant fraction; Thornvale's size selection discarded most of it ([[halo-pilot-panel-results-2026-04]], [[halo-interim-benchmark-report-2026-10]]).
- Nucleosomal cell-line material: closer, with a 167 bp peak, but end motifs reflect one cell line's nuclease environment rather than plasma. Our scores on it were low but not clearly interpretable.
- Literature on nucleosomal reference materials tests mutation detection, not fragment features ([[hong-2026-nucleosomal-egfr-reference-materials]]).

## Current Answer

Partial: sheared DNA is out; nucleosomal material is unproven. Until shown otherwise, fragmentomics assays are scored only on plasma tiers, as agreed at the [[halo-annual-meeting-2026-06-10]].

## What Would Settle It

A commutability study proper: the same patient plasma and candidate materials run side by side on both fragment assays, with the difference in bias analysed as in [[vierbaum-2026-eqa-material-commutability]]. [[tomasz-wilk]] and [[fatima-benali]] want to propose this for a 2027 batch.
