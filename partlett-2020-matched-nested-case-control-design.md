# Application of the matched nested case-control design to the secondary analysis of trial data.

## Summary

A worked example of a matched nested case-control study embedded in a randomised trial of feeding in growth-restricted preterm infants. The authors explain why the design is efficient, how risk-set matching makes cases and controls comparable at the time of the event, and how it lets exposure history be compared at the moment the outcome occurs.

## Why It Matters

Ines circulated it before the [[northvale-kickoff-2025-02-10|kick-off]] as the clearest short explanation of risk-set matching for people in the group who had only seen case-control studies with hospital controls. It became the reference for the design section of [[northvale-matching-specification-v2]].

## Key Details

The paper's point that exposure must be defined as of the case's event date, for controls too, is the reason our controls inherit their case's index date ([[northvale-date-definitions]]).

## Caveats

Clinical trial setting and a short-term outcome; storage and lead-time issues do not arise.
