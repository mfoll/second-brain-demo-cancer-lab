# Dependent Aims Risk

A grant aim carries dependent-aims risk when it can only start, or only has material to analyse, if another aim of the same application succeeds first. Reviewers read such an aim as a liability: one failure takes two aims down.

## How to Recognise It

- The aim has no hypothesis of its own; it says "we will apply X to the samples from aims 1 and 2".
- It competes with another aim for a finite resource: samples, plasma volume, a single postdoc's time.
- Its timeline starts at the month another aim ends.

## What to Do Instead

- Merge it into the aim it depends on as a work package with its own question, as happened with proteomics in the [[aurora-programme-grant-2027]] ([[aurora-mock-panel-2026-09-29]]).
- Give it an independent sample source or dataset, so it can proceed if the other aim slips.
- Or state the dependency and a fallback plainly, if the dependency is the point of the programme.

## Where It Came From

[[jonas-eriksen]] named it at the Aurora mock panel. [[rosa-lindgren]] had pointed to the Foundation's 2025 panel summary, where dependent aims were the most common weakness. The [[frontier-starting-grant]] avoided it by accident: its two work packages used different tissue blocks.

## Limits

Some programmes are sequential by nature, for example discovery then validation. There the dependency is unavoidable and the fix is a credible fallback, not a merge.
