# Research Engineers Deserve Authorship

## Stance

When a result depends on a pipeline that a research engineer built or adapted for it, the engineer is an author, not an acknowledgement. The same holds for data stewards whose work made a dataset usable.

## Scope

Applies to group papers and preprints. Routine use of a stable released pipeline years later does not by itself create authorship; a citation of the release does.

## Why I Think This

- The [[mrd-manuscript]] could not have been produced on the timeline without `varga-cfdna`, and its methods section is largely a description of [[tobias-wren]]'s work.
- Careers in research engineering depend on visible outputs. If we want people like Tobias to stay in research, credit has to be real.
- It keeps engineers involved in the science, which improves the code.

## What Would Change My Mind

If the institute or funders create recognised outputs for research software (citable releases counted in evaluations) that serve engineers better than authorship, I would rather use those.

## Implications

Authorship is discussed at project start, including engineering and stewardship roles, and recorded in the project note ([[authorship-and-credit-guidelines]]). Pipelines get citable releases as well.
