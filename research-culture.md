# Research Culture

How the group works together: meetings, credit, hiring, onboarding, feedback, and the norms that make a 15-person computational and wet-lab team function. Notes here are mostly practices and stances rather than results.

## Working Position

- Meetings exist to decide. The fortnightly lab meeting ends with a written list of decisions and owners ([[lab-meeting-format]], [[lab-meetings-should-end-with-decisions]]).
- Credit follows contribution, including engineering and data stewardship ([[authorship-and-credit-guidelines]], [[research-engineers-deserve-authorship]]).
- We hire for collaboration and evidence of finishing work ([[hire-for-collaboration-over-pedigree]]).
- New members get a named buddy, a first-week checklist and a 30-day check-in ([[onboard-a-new-lab-member]]).
- Knowledge is written down so that nobody is the only one who can fix something ([[software-bus-factor]], [[who-owns-what]]).

## How The Practices Changed

The group grew from 9 to 15 people between 2024 and 2026 without changing how it met or shared work. Through 2025 the fortnightly meeting was a round of updates ([[lab-meeting-format-2025]]), onboarding depended on whoever had time, and pipeline knowledge stayed with [[tobias-wren]]. Two events made the cost visible: [[felix-achterberg]]'s departure in August 2025, after which his single-cell data took weeks to locate and led to [[offboard-a-leaver]], and the 2026 survey, where only 4 of 13 members said meetings ended with clear decisions ([[retreat-2026-pre-survey]]).

The [[group-retreat-2026]] turned those signals into three changes: the split lab meeting, review of every pipeline change, and authorship conversations at project start. All three are recorded as rules in the [[lab-handbook]] and will be reviewed in January 2027, not assumed to work.
