# Paired Pre-Post Treatment Design

A study design in which the same lesion is sampled before and after treatment, so each lesion is its own control. It separates change caused by treatment from differences between patients and lesions.

## In the Atlas

Each of the 40 metastases in the [[colorectal-metastasis-atlas]] has a pre-chemotherapy core biopsy and the post-chemotherapy resection ([[atlas-tissue-manifest]]). Comparisons are within pair first, then pooled.

## Strengths

- Controls for patient-level and lesion-level variation, which is large in liver metastases.
- Lets us ask whether a subclone or niche present before treatment is enriched after it ([[chemotherapy-induced-clonal-selection]]).

## Weaknesses We Live With

- The two samples differ in size and type: a 1 mm core against a resection section. Absence in the biopsy is weak evidence of absence in the lesion ([[are-pre-chemo-core-biopsies-representative]]).
- Biopsy tissue is older in the archive and has lower RNA quality ([[tissue-section-qc]]).
- Responders with complete pathological response leave little tumour to profile after treatment; they inform niche changes, not clones.
- Selection: only patients whose metastases became resectable are included, so the atlas over-represents responders.

[[shi-2024-crlm-evolution-under-chemotherapy]] used a related paired multi-omics design in liver metastases under bevacizumab-based chemotherapy.
