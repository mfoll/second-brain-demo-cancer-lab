# Mock Panels Are Worth The Time

## Stance

Every application above about 500 k EUR should go through a mock panel at least eight weeks before the deadline, scored on the funder's own criteria by people who were not involved in writing it.

## Scope

Large, multi-aim applications where structure matters as much as science. Less useful for small project grants or fellowship renewals, where a single critical reader is enough.

## Why I Think This

- The [[aurora-mock-panel-2026-09-29]] changed the shape of the Aurora application from three aims to two. Ines and Marta had both sensed the problem but had been writing around it for three months.
- The Frontier practice interview with [[jonas-eriksen]] and [[amara-okonkwo]] anticipated every question but one at the [[frontier-interview-2025-10-14]].
- Scoring on the real criteria forces the panel to say where points are lost (approach and value for money, for Aurora) rather than offer general praise.
- [[leah-ostrander]] has held this view for years; her experience chairing charity panels is that structural flaws are rarely fixed after the first full draft.

## What Would Change My Mind

If a mock panel led us to restructure and the real panel then criticised exactly the new structure. Or if mock panels started costing more than a day of colleague time per application.

## Implications

Book the panel when the Project opens, not when the draft is ready. Send the aims page and a short case for support, not the full application. Reciprocate: Ines owes Jonas a mock panel in spring 2027.
