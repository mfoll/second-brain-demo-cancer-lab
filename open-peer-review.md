# Open Peer Review

An umbrella term for practices that make parts of peer review visible. The term is used loosely, so it helps to separate the components.

## Components

- Open reports: review reports, and often author responses, published with the paper.
- Open identities: reviewers' names disclosed to authors, readers, or both.
- Open participation: comments from people other than invited reviewers.
- Open pre-review: manuscripts posted as preprints before or during review.

A journal can adopt any subset. Genome Methods Letters' trial from 2027-01 combines open reports by author consent with open identities by reviewer opt-in ([[editorial-board-meeting-2026-05-27]]).

## Why The Separation Matters

Arguments for and against usually attach to one component. Published reports make the reasoning behind a decision visible and give reviewers credit for the work; disclosed identities raise accountability but expose junior reviewers to retaliation. Ines's stance, in [[open-peer-review-should-publish-reports-not-names]], rests on this split.
