# Report Lead-Time Windows, Not Pooled Estimates

## Stance

A prediagnostic biomarker study should present its results by time-to-diagnosis window as the main finding. A single pooled odds ratio, even with an early-year exclusion, should be secondary and never appear alone in an abstract.

## Scope

Single-sample prediagnostic studies of markers that could reflect existing disease: cfDNA, tumour markers, inflammation. For stable risk factors such as germline variants, a pooled estimate is fine.

## Why I Think This

- In Northvale the pooled estimate (1.48 per SD) is a blend of a strong effect within two years and none beyond five ([[northvale-window-sensitivity-tables]]). No reader can recover that from one number.
- Published studies that report by time before diagnosis tell a much more useful story ([[patel-2026-prediagnostic-methylation-cps3]], [[safizadeh-2026-inflammation-colorectal-reverse-causality]]).
- The choice of exclusion window becomes less contentious when the windows are on the table; Mei-Lin's disagreement over 6 months versus 1 year matters less ([[northvale-analysis-meeting-2026-06-24]]).

## What Would Change My Mind

Journals or reviewers consistently misreading window tables, or a field-standard continuous approach that is as easy to read.

## Implications

The symposium abstract gives no pooled estimate ([[northvale-lead-time-symposium-abstract]]); the paper's main table and figure are the window forest plot.
