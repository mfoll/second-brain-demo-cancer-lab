# Hire For Collaboration Over Pedigree

## Stance

For postdocs, evidence that someone finishes work with others and talks honestly about what went wrong predicts success in this group better than the names of their previous lab or journals.

## Why I Think This

- Our projects are shared across wet lab, computation, hospitals and a consortium. People who work well across those boundaries carry projects; brilliant solo workers stall them.
- In 2026 the advert asked for a statement on "one analysis you are proud of and what went wrong in it" ([[postdoc-job-advert-2026]]). It separated candidates more clearly than CVs did.
- The team session, scored as one criterion ([[postdoc-interview-scoring-sheet]]), was the panel's most informative input.

## What Would Change My Mind

If hires selected this way repeatedly struggle with the technical depth the projects need. One hire is not evidence either way; I will look again after [[owen-mcallister]]'s first year.

## Implications

- Criteria fixed before applications open, weighted toward collaboration and finishing.
- Read CVs for contribution statements, not journal names.
- Give unsuccessful candidates specific feedback; several, like [[mateo-silva]], are people we may work with later.
