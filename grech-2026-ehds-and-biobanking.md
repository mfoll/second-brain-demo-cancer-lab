# The European Health Data Space and biobanking in Europe: synergies, tensions and the future governance of data-driven health research

Grech L, Pace NP. Frontiers in Genetics, 2026.

## Summary

A review of how large-scale health data infrastructure for secondary use and consented biobanks could work together. The authors argue that they are complementary but not automatically interoperable: biobanks give depth (specimens, assays, longitudinal cohorts), population health data gives breadth. Obstacles they list include uneven digital maturity, weak representation of biospecimen and omics metadata in clinical data models, and unresolved interactions between opt-out mechanisms and biobank consent.

## Why It Matters

[[paolo-ferrante]] sent it to the board list in October 2026. The consent point is the one that matters for us: the [[corvane-biobank]] relies on 2004 to 2009 Northvale consent, and any future institute policy that assumes broad secondary use has to sit on top of that consent, not replace it. Background reading for the 2027 policy review rather than for draft 4.

## Caveats

A narrative review with a regional regulatory frame; we cite it for the structure of the argument, not for legal requirements.
