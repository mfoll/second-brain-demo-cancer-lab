# Cancer Early Detection

Retrieval surface for detecting cancer before it would be diagnosed clinically: blood-based signals in people without known disease, their timing, and the traps in interpreting them.

## How the group touches it

The group does not develop a screening test. Its contribution is measurement: how early a cfDNA methylation signal appears before diagnosis in a population cohort ([[northvale-early-detection-study]]), and what that means for anyone who would build a test. The MRD work ([[minimal-residual-disease]]) shares assays and pitfalls but asks a different question, recurrence after treatment.

## Key concepts

- [[lead-time]]: how far ahead of clinical diagnosis a signal appears.
- [[overdiagnosis]]: why earlier detection is not automatically benefit.
- [[time-to-diagnosis-windows]] and [[reverse-causation-in-prediagnostic-studies]]: how prediagnostic studies avoid fooling themselves.

## What we have learned so far

From the Northvale interim results ([[northvale-interim-results-2026-10]]): a methylation signal is clearly present within two years of diagnosis, faint at two to five years and absent beyond five. That agrees with the large published biobank study ([[patel-2026-prediagnostic-methylation-cps3]]) and is far more modest than early claims of detection four years ahead. For pancreatic cancer, where a short window may still matter clinically, the debate between a 6-month and a 1-year exclusion ([[how-do-we-handle-reverse-causation-in-the-northvale-lead-time-analysis]]) is really a debate about how short a useful lead time can be.

## Boundaries

The group will not make screening claims ([[prediagnostic-detection-is-not-screening-evidence]]). Requests from industry contacts to use Northvale results as test validation go to Ines and Mei-Lin, and are declined under the [[northvale-collaboration-agreement]] unless the steering group approves a new question.
