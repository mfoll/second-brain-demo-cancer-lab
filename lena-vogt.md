# Lena Vogt

Senior editor on the board of Genome Methods Letters and Ines's closest colleague on it. External, no affiliation hub in the vault.

## Role

Lena handles statistical genetics and methods submissions; Ines takes cfDNA, spatial and pipeline papers. When a submission straddles both, they agree who handles it and the other acts as a reviewer only if no conflict applies. Lena chairs the board's working group on peer review transparency.

## How We Work Together

- Board calls every quarter; the latest substantive one was [[editorial-board-meeting-2026-05-27]], where Lena's proposal for a published-reports trial was accepted.
- Short email exchanges when a handling decision is borderline. Lena prefers a clear recommendation with one sentence of reasoning over a long memo.
- She asked Ines in 2026-06 to draft the reviewer guidance for the trial, which is now a Next Action on [[peer-review-and-editorial]].

## Where We Differ

Lena wants signed reviews to be the default in the trial, with an opt-out. Ines wants published reports with signature as opt-in, mainly to protect early-career reviewers who review senior authors' work. The compromise adopted on 2026-05-27 was opt-in signatures for the first year with a review of uptake in 2028. Ines's reasoning is in [[open-peer-review-should-publish-reports-not-names]], and the open question for her own practice is [[should-i-sign-my-peer-review-reports]].

## Current Threads

- Reviewer guidance draft for the trial, due 2026-11-30.
- Lena asked whether the group would review a Mendelian randomization methods paper; Ines declined because nobody in the group is expert enough, and pointed her to [[hiroshi-tanabe]].

## Board Terms

Both serve terms to 2028-02; see [[genome-methods-letters-editorial-board-terms]].

## Handling Arrangement

When a paper falls between them, the rule they settled in 2025 is simple: whoever has the closer methodological expertise handles it, and the other is not asked to review it, to avoid the appearance of the board reviewing its own decisions. Disputes go to the editor-in-chief; none so far.
