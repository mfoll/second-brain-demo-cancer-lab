# Lessons From The 2025 Pilots

Ines's comparison of the three technology pilots run in 2025, written for the 2026 retreat ([[group-retreat-2026]]).

| Pilot | Months | Stop rule written | Outcome | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [[visium-hd-pilot]] | 6 | At kickoff | Adopted with 8 µm bins | About 38,000 EUR |
| [[single-cell-cnv-pilot]] | 7 | Month 3 | Not adopted | About 46,000 EUR |
| [[exosome-rna-pilot]] | 4.5 | Never | Superseded by the methylation panel | About 14,000 EUR |

## What Worked

- Running pilots on existing material (archived blocks, banked plasma, tumours with bulk data) kept them cheap and fast.
- Comparing against the incumbent method, not against nothing. The single-cell pilot was useful because it had bulk trees on the same tumours.
- A decision meeting with the evidence on one page, followed by a Project status change the same week.

## What Did Not

- Without a stop rule, the exosome pilot discussion drifted to yield optimisation.
- Two pilots ran with one person each; when [[felix-achterberg]] announced his departure, the single-cell pilot had to close on his timeline rather than ours. Pair every pilot with a second person who can finish it.
- Parallel pilots for the same question (exosome RNA and methylation for the MRD study) made the comparison easy, but only because the methylation work was already funded.

## For 2027

Any new technology pilot (candidate: imaging-based panels for the atlas follow-up) gets a stop rule, a second person, and a decision date in its Project note on day one.
