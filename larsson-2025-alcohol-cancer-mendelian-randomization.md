# Alcohol consumption and risk of cancer: a Mendelian randomization analysis of four biobanks and consortium data.

Larsson SC et al., BMC Medicine, 2025.

## Summary

MR of genetically predicted alcohol consumption and 20 cancers, using 95 variants and four large biobanks plus cancer-specific consortia. No association with overall cancer. Among cancers with strong observational evidence, a significant positive estimate for head and neck cancer after multiple-testing correction, nominal positive estimates for colorectal and oesophageal cancer, and null estimates for breast cancer, and an unexpected inverse estimate for kidney cancer.

## Why It Matters

Ines brought it to journal club on 2026-04-02 as a cautionary example: a well-established carcinogen gives a patchy MR picture across sites. That is not evidence against the carcinogen; it shows how instrument strength, outcome case numbers and pleiotropy shape MR results. It underpins her stance in [[mendelian-randomization-is-triangulation-not-a-verdict]] and the expectation that a CH MR could be inconclusive for most sites.

## Caveats

Alcohol instruments include strong single variants with known pleiotropy; the analogy with CH is about method, not biology.
