# Are Pre-Chemo Core Biopsies Representative?

Does a single 1 mm core biopsy taken before chemotherapy capture the subclonal and microenvironmental makeup of the metastasis well enough to compare with the resection after treatment?

## Why It Matters

The atlas's central claim type, "this subclone or niche was enriched by chemotherapy", rests on the biopsy being a fair sample. If it is not, apparent selection may just be sampling.

## What We Know

- [[benedikt-kraus]] raised it at the start: liver metastases are spatially heterogeneous and a core often hits the periphery.
- Batch 1 and 2: in 9 of 26 pairs the resection shows an inferred copy-number subclone absent from the biopsy. Whether these are new or missed cannot be told from one core.
- Two patients in the clinical archive have untreated resections of a synchronous second metastasis, which could serve as a heterogeneity reference.

## Options

- Report subclone appearances as "not detected before" rather than "new", with a sensitivity analysis restricted to biopsies with at least 30% tumour content.
- Simulate core sampling on the resection sections themselves: draw 1 mm virtual cores and measure how often each subclone is missed.

The virtual-core simulation is cheap and is on Owen's starting list ([[owen-atlas-onboarding-plan]]).
