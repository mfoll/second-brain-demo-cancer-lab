# Should I Sign My Peer Review Reports?

Ines's own practice question, separate from journal policy: when a journal offers the option, should she sign her reviews?

## Context

She argued at the [[editorial-board-meeting-2026-05-27]] that signatures should be opt-in to protect junior reviewers. As a group leader she is not in that position, and [[lena-vogt]] pointed out that senior reviewers signing is what makes signing normal.

## Current Thinking

- First signed report in 2026-09, for a methods paper where the critique was technical and the report constructive ([[review-report-spatial-omics-journal-2026-09]]).
- Leaning towards signing by default, except where she recommends rejection of work by early-career first authors, or where she has a competing project.
- Unresolved whether signing changes how she writes. She suspects it makes her more careful and slower.

## What Would Answer It

A year of signing where offered, then a look at whether her reports or acceptance rate changed. To be reviewed with the 2027 log ([[peer-review-log]]). The trial guidance she is drafting for the board is due 2026-11-30 and will force a public position sooner.
