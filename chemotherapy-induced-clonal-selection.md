# Chemotherapy-Induced Clonal Selection

The idea that chemotherapy changes a tumour mainly by selecting pre-existing resistant clones, as opposed to inducing new changes or remodelling the microenvironment around unchanged clones. In practice all three happen; the atlas asks which dominates in treated liver metastases.

## Three Readings of a Post-Treatment Sample

- Selection: a subclone present before treatment is enriched after it. Predicts shared copy-number profiles with shifted fractions.
- Induction or drift: new changes appear after treatment. Hard to separate from sampling a part of the lesion the biopsy missed.
- Niche remodelling: tumour clones are similar but surrounding cells change (myeloid, fibroblast, hepatocyte damage). [[shi-2024-crlm-evolution-under-chemotherapy]] reported that most liver metastases changed little genomically under treatment while expression changed a lot, which points this way.

## How the Atlas Tests It

Spatial copy-number subclones inferred from expression in paired sections ([[can-spatial-cnv-replace-single-cell-dna]]), niche composition by deconvolution ([[cell-type-deconvolution]]), stratified by [[tumour-regression-grade]]. The core-biopsy limitation applies throughout ([[are-pre-chemo-core-biopsies-representative]]).

Clinical cfDNA monitoring shows such dynamics can be fast: [[simhadri-2024-braf-colon-clonal-dynamics]] followed a resistant clone appearing and clearing under alternating therapies in one patient.
