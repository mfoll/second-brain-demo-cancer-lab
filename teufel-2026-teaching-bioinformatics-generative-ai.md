# Teaching bioinformatics with generative AI: judgment, uncertainty, and responsibility

Teufel AI, Liberles DA. Journal of Microbiology & Biology Education, 2026.

## Summary

A perspective from instructors of undergraduate and graduate bioinformatics courses on teaching in the early generative AI period. The authors argue that prohibiting AI use is increasingly impractical and that instructors should rethink what they teach when routine coding and analysis can be automated. Their central point is that AI is most useful to people who can judge its output, which is exactly what students are still learning. Deliberate integration can lower technical barriers; unscaffolded use can hide misunderstanding and skip foundational skills.

## Why It Matters

Read for the open question [[how-should-the-course-handle-generative-ai-in-assessments]] after the 2026 liquid biopsy practical, where students submitted code they could not explain. It supports keeping the oral defence of methods and adding an exercise where students critique generated code, rather than a ban.

## Caveats

Opinion piece; no evaluation of specific assessment designs.
