# Non-invasive early detection of cancer four years before conventional diagnosis using a blood test.

## Summary

Preliminary results of a targeted cfDNA methylation test applied to stored plasma from a large longitudinal cohort. In 605 asymptomatic participants, 191 of whom developed one of five common cancers within four years of blood draw, the test reported very high detection among people later diagnosed, alongside high sensitivity and specificity in post-diagnosis patients. The authors note that larger longitudinal studies are needed.

## Why It Matters

This is the paper that made the Northvale study look feasible, and Ines cited it in the original pitch to [[mei-lin-zhou]]. It is also the reason the group is careful: its headline detection rate in prediagnostic samples is far above what later, larger studies such as [[patel-2026-prediagnostic-methylation-cps3]] report. Our design differs in matching controls on draw date and storage, and in reporting by [[time-to-diagnosis-windows]].

## Caveats

Small number of later-diagnosed cases per cancer type; how controls were selected relative to storage time matters for any comparison with our results.
