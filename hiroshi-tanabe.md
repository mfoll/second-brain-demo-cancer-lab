# Hiroshi Tanabe

Statistical geneticist who develops and teaches Mendelian randomization methods, with particular interest in pleiotropy-robust estimators and selection bias in cohort studies. External contact, no affiliation hub.

## How We Met

[[lucas-brandt]] met him at a genetic epidemiology course in 2025 and introduced him to Ines in 2025-11, a month after the [[ch-cancer-risk-analysis]] started. His annotated reading list became the backbone of the [[mendelian-randomization]] cluster ([[mr-reading-list-hiroshi-tanabe]]).

## What He Has Told Us

- CH is a hard exposure for MR. Germline instruments for CH are few, mostly weak, and several sit in genes with plausible direct effects on cancer risk (TERT region, DNA repair genes). He expects pleiotropy to dominate unless we restrict to instruments with a clear haematopoietic mechanism. See [[pleiotropy]] and [[ch-instrument-shortlist]].
- He recommends treating MR as one leg of triangulation with the cohort hazard ratios, not as a test that settles causality. Ines agrees ([[mendelian-randomization-is-triangulation-not-a-verdict]]).
- One-sample MR in a 6,000-person subcohort would be underpowered for most single cancers; two-sample designs with public summary statistics are the realistic option, with Northvale as a replication. This is the crux of [[can-mendelian-randomization-test-ch-as-a-cause-of-cancer]].
