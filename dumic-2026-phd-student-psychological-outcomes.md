# Psychological Outcomes and Research Engagement Among PhD Students in Medicine and Health Sciences: A Systematic Review

Dumic L, Lovrincevic Pavlovic N, Miskulin M. Behavioral Sciences, 2026.

## Summary

A systematic review of 27 studies on burnout, impostor feelings, research engagement and related distress among PhD students in medicine and health sciences, many of whom combine doctoral work with clinical duties. The authors report that distress was linked more to institutional factors than to personal traits, that burnout was common and associated with professional isolation and poor relationships with supervisors, and that research engagement did not seem to protect against burnout. Impostor feelings were a recurring theme but rarely measured with a validated instrument.

## Why It Matters

Ines read it in October 2026 while preparing [[sofia-thesis-committee]]. It supports the structure in [[supervision-meeting-structure]]: regular contact and attention to isolation matter more than enthusiasm for the project. The finding that engagement does not protect against burnout is a useful corrective; a motivated student can still be in trouble.

## Caveats

Narrative synthesis of heterogeneous studies; most participants were in clinical programmes, not computational groups like ours.
