# Thursday 2 April 2026

## Classifier design: the March decision partly reversed

At the [[classifier-design-review-2026-04-02|classifier design review]] we revisited the decision of the [[lab-meeting-2026-03-12|12 March lab meeting]] to drop gradient boosting from the [[fragmentomics-classifier|Fragmentomics Classifier]]. That decision was only half right.

Sofia split the evaluation by feature family. The batch problem sits in the fragment-length features; the end-motif features barely move between kit lots, and gradient boosting gets more out of them than logistic regression does. So the classifier becomes a stacked model: regularised logistic regression on the [[fragment-length-and-end-motif-features|fragment-length features]], gradient boosting kept for the end-motif features, and a simple logistic layer combining the two. The length features get a bridging-sample correction, which uses samples extracted on both lots and no outcome labels. That is why it is acceptable where ComBat-style correction was not.

The stacked model holds up best when whole batches are left out. The numbers are in the [[classifier-stacking-evaluation-2026-04|stacking evaluation]] and the design in the [[classifier-model-card-v2|model card v2]].

## Why accept the extra complexity

Maja asked whether a small gain justified two models. My answer was yes: the gain is in the setting that matters for external validation, and a stacked model degrades more gracefully if one family fails at a new site. Dmitri had suspected in March that the motif family was clean; he was right.

## What changes and what stays

The March rationale stays on record: no gradient boosting on fragment-length features. Every retraining reports leave-batch-out performance, and the manuscript revision will describe the stacked model. Arjun's point about three extraction chemistries across Halo sites is now an open question: [[does-the-stacked-classifier-generalise-across-extraction-batches|Does the stacked classifier generalise across extraction batches?]].
