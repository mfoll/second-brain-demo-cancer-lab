# What Lead Time Over Imaging Is Clinically Meaningful?

Our serial cfDNA call precedes radiological recurrence by a median of 4.3 months in St Aldric. How much lead time, in which patients, would actually change care?

## Why It Matters

Raised by [[samuel-okoye|Samuel Okoye]] at the [[mrd-interim-analysis-meeting-2025-12-03|December 2025 interim meeting]] and echoed by patients at the [[patient-advisory-meeting-2025-10-22|patient advisory meeting]]. It shapes how the [[mrd-manuscript|MRD Manuscript]] frames its claim and whether a follow-up interventional study is worth proposing.

## Current Thinking

- Lead time is only useful if an action follows: earlier second-line chemotherapy, trial eligibility while performance status is better, or local treatment of oligometastatic recurrence.
- Samuel's estimate: lead time below about two months rarely changes anything given scan scheduling; three to six months could matter for fit patients.
- The 4.3-month median is against three-monthly CT and is interval-censored ([[molecular-lead-time]]); Reviewer 3 asked for a censoring-aware estimate, which will likely shorten it.
- Published pancreatic MRD work supports prognostic value but not yet benefit from acting earlier ([[theocharopoulos-2025-mrd-pdac-perioperative]], [[ojha-2025-ctdna-pancreatic-critical-appraisal]]).

## What Would Answer It

Not this study. An interventional trial would. For now, the manuscript reports lead time as a property of the assay, not as a clinical benefit.
