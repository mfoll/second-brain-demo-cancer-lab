# Felix Achterberg

Former postdoc (2023 to August 2025) who ran the [[single-cell-cnv-pilot]]. He left in August 2025 for a computational role in industry. Former members keep their group affiliation in this vault.

## Role While in the Group

- Designed and ran the single-cell CNV pilot: nuclei preparation with the wet lab, low-pass sequencing through the core, copy-number calling and tree comparison against our multi-region bulk phylogenies ([[multi-region-bulk-phylogeny]]).
- Wrote the [[single-cell-cnv-pilot-report]] and the [[single-cell-cnv-cost-comparison]] that the decision rested on.
- Maintained the group's bulk phylogeny scripts before they moved into the Nextflow pipelines.

## Handover

Before leaving he archived the raw and processed pilot data with [[yara-castellanos]] ([[single-cell-cnv-pilot-data]]) and recorded the environment as a container recipe. The handover was clean: the report states exactly which runs fed which figure.

## Current Contact

Felix is happy to answer questions about the pilot data by email, at a low rate. If the single-cell question is reopened (cost below about 3,000 EUR per tumour, or a study needing subclones below 5%), he should be asked first and offered co-authorship on anything that uses his data.

## What He Left Behind

Beyond the pilot, Felix's lasting contribution is the habit of writing down what "adds value" means before a pilot reaches its decision. When the single-cell pilot had no stop rule after three months, he asked for one; that conversation became [[pilots-need-a-written-stop-rule]]. His scripts for comparing trees from different methods are still used when checking atlas copy-number subclones against bulk data.

## Boundaries

Felix now works for a company that sells sequencing services. Do not share unpublished atlas or benchmark results with him beyond what concerns his own pilot data, and route any collaboration proposal through the institute's technology transfer office first.
