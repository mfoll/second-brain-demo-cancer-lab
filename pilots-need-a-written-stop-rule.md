# Pilots Need A Written Stop Rule

## Stance

Every pilot starts with a short written rule: what result means adopt, what means stop, and by when. Without it, pilots drift into small projects that nobody closes.

## Why I Think This

The three 2025 pilots behaved differently. The [[visium-hd-pilot]] had a written rule from the start (median above 100 UMIs per 8 µm bin in resections, invasive margin recoverable) and closed on time. The [[single-cell-cnv-pilot]] had a rule only after month three, added when [[felix-achterberg]] asked what "adds to bulk" meant; it then closed cleanly. The [[exosome-rna-pilot]] had none, and for two months the discussion was about improving yield rather than about whether the signal could ever add sensitivity ([[exosome-rna-pilot-plan]]).

## Scope

Technology and method pilots under about six months. Exploratory analyses of existing data need less formality.

## What Would Change My Mind

A pilot that found something valuable only because it was allowed to wander past its stop rule.

## Implications

Pilot Project notes record the rule under Outcome. See [[lessons-from-the-2025-pilots]].
