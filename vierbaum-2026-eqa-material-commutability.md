# Using native human sera to assess the suitability of external quality assessment materials based on testosterone, progesterone, and 17β-estradiol measurements.

Vierbaum L, Kaiser P, Keller T, et al. Frontiers in Molecular Biosciences, 2026.

## Summary

External quality assessment schemes usually send processed control materials, whose behaviour may differ from patient samples. This study added a few native human sera to a regular EQA survey for three steroid hormones and compared laboratories' bias on native versus processed materials with a mixed-effects model. Where the difference in bias between sample types exceeded a strict criterion, the authors tried to separate non-commutability of the material from calibration problems of the measurement procedure. The two-stage approach found the processed materials suitable for some procedure pairings and revealed calibration or specificity issues in others.

## Why It Matters

Far from cfDNA, but it is the cleanest worked example we found of the design we want: run native and processed material side by side and use the difference to separate a material problem from an assay problem. The truth set does this, in a small way, with sheared versus nucleosomal DNA and with the patient tier ([[commutability-of-reference-materials]]). Found by Ines in July while writing up the pilot.

## Caveats

Hormone immunoassays and mass spectrometry, not sequencing. The statistical approach transfers; the numbers do not.
