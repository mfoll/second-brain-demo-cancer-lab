# Daniel Asante

Senior statistician at the [[northern-cancer-registry]]. He is the registry's technical contact for our linkages and the person who explains what the registry's numbers mean and do not mean. External to Corvane; no affiliation hub in this vault.

## Role in our work

Daniel ran both Northvale linkages: the original extract with follow-up to 2023 ([[northern-cancer-registry-extract-2023]]) and the 2025 extract delivered on 2026-09-08 under the institute [[registry-linkage-agreement]] ([[northern-cancer-registry-extract-2025]]). He reviewed our [[registry-linkage-variable-specification]] before the request went in and caught that we had asked for date of death without cause of death, which the [[ch-cancer-risk-analysis]] needs to separate cancer from non-cancer deaths.

He is the source of our working figures on [[registry-reporting-lag]]: about 95% of cases for a calendar year are registered twelve months after year end, and above 99% after 24 months. Pancreatic cancers diagnosed only at death certificate are added later still.

## How we work together

- Joins the Northvale analysis meeting when registry items are on the agenda; he attended [[northvale-analysis-meeting-2026-10-14]] to walk through the control replacements.
- Prefers questions in writing with record identifiers in a secure transfer, never in email. Pseudonymised identifiers only.
- Turnaround on a query is about two weeks; extract production is about eight weeks from an approved request.

## Current threads

- Whether the 97 new pancreatic and colorectal cases in the 2025 extract are final or will still move (he expects a few diagnosis-date changes in the 2027 refresh).
- Scheduling a 2027 refresh under the agreement.
- Advice on [[does-registry-lag-misclassify-recent-northvale-controls]].

## History

The first Northvale linkage took about six months through a cohort-specific approval. Daniel argued in 2025 for an institute-level agreement covering all Corvane linkages, which became the [[registry-linkage-agreement]]; the 2025 extract under it took eight weeks. He is careful to separate registry facts from registry estimates: completeness figures are his professional judgement, not published statistics, and he asks that they be described that way.
