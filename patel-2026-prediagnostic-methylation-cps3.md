# Early cancer detection in a large American Cancer Society cohort study: Retrospective analysis of methylated DNA biomarkers.

## Summary

A cfDNA methylation multi-cancer test applied to biobanked plasma from a very large prospective cohort, comparing participants diagnosed with cancer within three years of blood draw with participants who stayed cancer-free. No signals were called in about 1,370 non-cancer participants. Among about 1,380 later cases, detectability fell steeply with time before diagnosis: roughly 15% one year before, 6% two years before and 2% three years before, and was much higher for cancers that later proved fatal. Mean detection preceded diagnosis by under a year.

## Why It Matters

The closest published analogue to the [[lead-time-analysis]], and it arrived (Maja flagged it on 2026-05-12) just before the reverse-causation discussion. The steep fall with time matches our window pattern and strengthened the case for reporting windows rather than one estimate at [[northvale-analysis-meeting-2026-06-24]]. Its lower numbers compared with [[chen-2020-panseer-four-years-before-diagnosis]] are a useful calibration when people ask why our signal beyond five years is null.

## Caveats

Commercial classifier with a fixed threshold; our analysis uses a continuous score in a matched design, so the numbers are not directly comparable.
