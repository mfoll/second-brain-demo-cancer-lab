# Peer Review And Editorial

Ines's service to the wider field: manuscript and grant reviewing, her seat on the editorial board of Genome Methods Letters, and organising scientific meetings, this year the [[tumour-evolution-symposium-2026|Tumour Evolution Symposium 2026]]. It has no end date; individual meetings and board terms are the finite pieces.

## Scope

- Manuscript reviews for journals, mostly from Genome Methods Letters, Annals of Liquid Biopsy, Spatial Omics Journal and Computational Oncology Review. Reports are logged in [[peer-review-log]]; manuscript content stays out of the vault.
- The Genome Methods Letters editorial board (term 2025-03 to 2028-02, see [[genome-methods-letters-editorial-board-terms]]), working mostly with [[lena-vogt]].
- Symposium and session organisation. The 2026 symposium is the only active Project.
- Not here: our own submissions and revisions. The [[mrd-manuscript]] answers to [[tumour-evolution-research]], and the invited [[mrd-review-article]] is a research output, not a service. Grant panels for funders we also apply to are declined, see [[review-workload-policy]].

Three reports this year are summarised generically in [[review-report-genome-methods-letters-2026-02]], [[review-report-annals-of-liquid-biopsy-2026-06]] and [[review-report-spatial-omics-journal-2026-09]].

- Conflict rule in force: no reviews of Halo consortium papers or of anything that cites the St Aldric cohort, and nothing handled by [[clara-hoffmann]] while the MRD manuscript is under revision at her journal.

## Active Projects and Workflows

- [[tumour-evolution-symposium-2026]]: two days, 2026-11-19/20, at Corvane, co-organised with [[oluwaseun-adebayo]].
- Review intake: requests arrive by email; Ines answers within three working days using the rules in [[review-workload-policy]] and adds accepted ones to [[peer-review-log]] with the due date.
- Board work: roughly two handling-editor assignments a month, plus the quarterly board call.

## Working Context

Ines treats reviewing as part of the job, not a favour, but caps it because the cost lands on the group: each manuscript review takes her four to six hours, and methods papers with code take longer because she tries to run the code. [[tobias-wren]] sometimes helps check containers or pipelines when a submission ships a Nextflow workflow; that is acknowledged in the report as "with technical assistance" and only with the editor's agreement.

Review requests that look like exploratory reading (a pathology foundation model paper, a Mendelian randomization study) are useful even when declined: the abstract often goes to the matching cluster under [[exploratory-reading]].
