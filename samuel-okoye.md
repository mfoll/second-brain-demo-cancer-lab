# Samuel Okoye

Consultant pancreatic surgeon at St Aldric University Hospital and clinical lead of the [[pancreatic-cfdna-mrd-study|Pancreatic cfDNA MRD Study]]. External collaborator, so no affiliation hub in this vault.

## Role

- Built the St Aldric post-resection plasma collection that became the [[st-aldric-mrd-cohort|St Aldric MRD cohort]] and holds the clinical data.
- Clinical PI under the St Aldric ethics approval ([[st-aldric-ethics-amendment-2025-05]]) and signatory of the data transfer to Corvane.
- Senior clinical author on the [[mrd-manuscript|MRD Manuscript]]; checks every clinical claim, especially imaging intervals and recurrence definitions.

## How We Work Together

Monthly MRD study meeting by video, plus ad hoc calls when clinical data change. Samuel's time is limited by theatre lists; send him short written questions with a deadline rather than open-ended requests, and route data requests through his research nurse via [[priya-raman|Priya Raman]]. He is direct about clinical relevance: at the [[mrd-interim-analysis-meeting-2025-12-03|December 2025 interim meeting]] he said that a lead time only matters if it would change management, which became the open question [[what-lead-time-over-imaging-is-clinically-meaningful]].

He also connected the group with [[anna-sorensen|Anna Sorensen]] for patient involvement.

## Current Threads

- Reviewer 3 on the manuscript questions the imaging schedule; Samuel is pulling the exact CT dates for interval-censored lead-time analysis.
- Supports a St Aldric follow-up study with fixed sampling for clinical utility, contingent on the revision.
- Wants CA19-9 reported alongside cfDNA ([[should-we-report-ca19-9-alongside-cfdna]]).

## Background

Samuel leads the pancreatic surgery service at St Aldric and started banking post-operative plasma in 2022 with a small local grant, before any analysis partner was in place. That foresight is why the study could start with two years of samples already in the freezer. He co-chairs the St Aldric hepatobiliary research meeting and is open to a prospective follow-up study with fixed sampling, but only with a clear clinical question.
