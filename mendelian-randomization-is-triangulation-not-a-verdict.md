# Mendelian Randomization Is Triangulation, Not A Verdict

## Stance

A Mendelian randomization result is one line of evidence with its own biases, to be set beside observational, experimental and biological evidence. It should not be presented as the test that settles whether an exposure is causal.

## Scope

Applies to how we would use MR in the [[ch-cancer-risk-analysis]] and how Ines reads MR papers in review. Not a judgement on MR's value; it is often the best tool available for an exposure we cannot randomise.

## Why I Think This

- The key assumption, no horizontal [[pleiotropy]], cannot be tested directly and is doubtful for many exposures, CH among them.
- Well-established causes can give patchy MR results across outcomes ([[larsson-2025-alcohol-cancer-mendelian-randomization]]), so a null MR does not clear an exposure.
- The flood of low-effort two-sample MR papers has made "MR shows causality" a weak claim in the eyes of reviewers.
- [[hiroshi-tanabe]], who builds the methods, says the same.

## What Would Change My Mind

Strong, specific instruments with well-understood biology, where robust methods agree and the result matches other evidence. Then MR could carry more weight on its own.

## Implications

- MR stays out of the first CH paper. If a follow-up MR is done, it is a pre-specified analysis with all robust methods reported, presented next to the cohort hazard ratios, not as the headline.
- In reviews, Ines asks MR authors for sensitivity analyses and for causal language proportionate to them.
