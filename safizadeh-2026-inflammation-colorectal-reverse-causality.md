# Systemic Inflammation and Colorectal Cancer: Assessing the Role of Reverse Causality.

## Summary

In a large population biobank with about 12 years of follow-up and roughly 5,000 incident colorectal cancers, baseline inflammation markers such as C-reactive protein were strongly associated with colorectal cancer in the first year after blood collection, and the association largely disappeared in later follow-up intervals. The authors conclude that part of the reported association reflects preclinical disease.

## Why It Matters

A clean, recent demonstration of the pattern we see with methylation, using time since sampling as the diagnostic. Maja found it on 2026-10-19 and will cite it in the lead-time paper's methods rationale for the 1-year exclusion. It was published after the [[northvale-analysis-meeting-2026-06-24|June decision]], so it supports the decision rather than having informed it.

## Caveats

Inflammation markers, not cfDNA; Cox models on the full cohort rather than a matched design.
