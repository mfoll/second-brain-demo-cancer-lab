# Overdiagnosis in breast cancer screening: the importance of length of observation period and lead time.

## Summary

A simulation of a breast screening programme in which screening has no effect other than advancing diagnosis (lead time averaging 40 months). With twenty years of observation, lead time alone produced an apparent excess incidence of 37%, which only fell below 10% with 25 years or more of follow-up. Short observation periods therefore overestimate overdiagnosis by counting early-diagnosed cancers as overdiagnosed.

## Why It Matters

Background for the [[lead-time]] and [[overdiagnosis]] concept notes, and for explaining to non-epidemiologists in the group why lead time and overdiagnosis are entangled. Read for the April 2026 journal club on lead time.

## Caveats

Screening context with a modelled population; Northvale does not screen anyone.
