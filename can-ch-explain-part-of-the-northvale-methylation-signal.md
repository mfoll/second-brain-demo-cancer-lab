# Can CH explain part of the Northvale methylation signal?

## Question

Most cfDNA in plasma comes from blood cells. Clonal hematopoiesis, especially DNMT3A and TET2 clones, changes the methylation of blood cells. Could CH in older participants raise the methylation score independently of cancer, and could CH be more common in future cases?

## Why it matters

If yes, part of the early-window signal could reflect host blood rather than tumour DNA, and the score would partly measure a risk factor rather than early disease. It connects the two uses of our CH calls ([[clonal-hematopoiesis]]).

## What we can do

- Only 86 participants are both in the case-control set and the CH subcohort (31 cases, 55 controls). [[northvale-sap-amendment-1]] adds a descriptive check in this overlap: score by CH status within controls.
- The panel's regions were chosen to avoid known blood-specific methylation ([[methylation-panel-design]]), which should limit the effect.
- Plasma-only CH classification ([[arango-argoty-2025-metach-cfdna-ch-variants]]) is for variants, not methylation, so it does not help directly.

## Current thinking

Unlikely to explain the window gradient, because CH prevalence rises slowly with age and does not change in the year before diagnosis. But the overlap check is underpowered. [[lucas-brandt]] owns the check; result expected with the re-run.
