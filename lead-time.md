# Lead Time

The interval by which a test or signal brings forward the diagnosis of a cancer compared with when it would have been diagnosed clinically. In screening it is the source of lead-time bias: survival measured from diagnosis looks longer even if death is not delayed.

## In a prediagnostic cohort study

Nobody is screened in Northvale. Here "lead time" means something narrower: how long before clinical diagnosis a signal is detectable in stored blood. It is estimated indirectly, from how the association changes across [[time-to-diagnosis-windows]]. Our interim results suggest a detectable signal within about two years and little beyond five; [[patel-2026-prediagnostic-methylation-cps3]] reported a similar steep fall-off.

## Traps

- Lead time is not one number. It varies by cancer, stage and test, and estimates depend on the method ([[benbassat-2021-lead-time-lung-screening]]).
- Lead time and [[overdiagnosis]] are entangled; short follow-up confuses them ([[duffy-2013-overdiagnosis-lead-time]]).
- With one sample per person, short lead time and reverse causation give the same pattern ([[reverse-causation-in-prediagnostic-studies]]).
- A lead time in stored samples is not evidence that earlier detection helps ([[prediagnostic-detection-is-not-screening-evidence]]).
