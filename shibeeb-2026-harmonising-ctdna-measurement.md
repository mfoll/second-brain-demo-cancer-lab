# Harmonising ctDNA Measurement in Haematological Malignancies: Traceability, Commutability and Reporting.

Shibeeb S. Diagnostics, 2026. Review.

## Summary

A review arguing that ctDNA results are hard to compare across laboratories because the measurand itself is defined differently (allele fraction versus mutant molecules per mL), because pre-analytical steps and workflow losses vary, and because clonal haematopoiesis confounds calls. It proposes a harmonisation framework built on three linked ideas: metrological traceability, commutability of reference materials, and fit-for-purpose reference materials that support calibration, quality control, external quality assessment and cut-offs for specific clinical uses.

## Why It Matters

The clearest statement we have of why commutability must be tested rather than assumed. Ines used its framing in the WP4 slides at the [[halo-annual-meeting-2026-06-10]] and it underpins our Concept note [[commutability-of-reference-materials]]. The point about the measurand also affected the [[halo-wp4-analysis-plan]] v1.1, which now reports mutant molecules per mL alongside VAF where assays allow.

## Caveats

Written for haematological malignancies; the CH point transfers to solid-tumour MRD directly, but the clinical use cases do not. A review, so no new data.
