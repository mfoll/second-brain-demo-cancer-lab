# Overdiagnosis

The diagnosis of a cancer that would never have caused symptoms or death during a person's lifetime if it had not been detected. It is not a false positive: the cancer is real. The harm is the diagnosis and treatment that brought no benefit.

## Why it matters for early detection work

Any test that finds cancer earlier will also find some cancers that did not need finding. More sensitive cfDNA tests make this more likely for slow-growing tumours. Overdiagnosis can only be estimated in populations offered testing and followed long enough ([[duffy-2013-overdiagnosis-lead-time]], [[barchuk-2025-overdiagnosis-evidence-categories]]).

## Relevance to Northvale

The [[northvale-early-detection-study]] cannot estimate overdiagnosis: its cases are cancers diagnosed clinically, by definition the ones that surfaced. This is a boundary for the paper's discussion, and part of the group's stance in [[prediagnostic-detection-is-not-screening-evidence]]. Pancreatic cancer is rarely overdiagnosed; colorectal cancer less rarely, through screening-detected early lesions, which is why stage at diagnosis is in the [[northvale-case-control-codebook]].
