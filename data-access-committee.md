# Data Access Committee

## Definition

A data access committee (DAC) is the body that decides who may use a controlled dataset, for what purpose and under which conditions. It checks requests against the consent, the governing agreements and the policy, and it records each decision so that use can be audited later.

## How It Differs From A Governance Board

A board sets policy, approves agreements and DPIAs, and handles exceptions. A DAC applies policy to individual requests, quickly and consistently. At Corvane the board has done both, which is why external requests took a median of 34 working days to decide in 2025 ([[data-request-statistics-2025]]).

## Our Planned Set-Up

[[data-access-policy-draft-3]] gives consortium uses a per-use review by a standing DAC function of the board: the chair, the data protection officer and one rotating group leader, with a 20-working-day target. The change to the [[data-governance-board-terms-of-reference]] goes to the board with the policy. Halo sites already run their own DACs; the [[halo-data-sharing-agreement-template]] expects each contributing site's committee to approve each use schedule.

## Things That Make A DAC Work

Drawn from [[lawson-2024-data-access-committee-survey]] and our own experience: a standard request form, published criteria, a target turnaround, a public log of approved uses, and a clear route for conflicts of interest. Members with an interest present but do not decide.
