# Cancer Genomics Course 2026

Third edition of the institute's PhD course in cancer genomics, coordinated by Ines. 24 students, 2026-01-12 to 2026-05-22.

## Outcome

Reached: all 24 students completed the course and 23 passed the final project on the first attempt; one resubmitted and passed in June.

## Current State

- Completed. Feedback from 19 of 24 students is in [[cancer-genomics-course-2026-feedback-survey]]; overall rating 4.3 out of 5, up from 3.9 in 2025.
- Changes for the fourth edition are collected in [[cancer-genomics-course-2027-changes]], to be confirmed by 2026-12-15.
- One open policy issue carries over: how to handle generative AI in assessed code ([[how-should-the-course-handle-generative-ai-in-assessments]]).

## Scope and Boundaries

The course is a graduate school commitment: 4 ECTS, Monday afternoons plus two practical weeks. Ines coordinates and teaches four sessions; group members teach the practicals. Course materials live in the [[course-teaching-materials-repository]]; student marks live in the graduate school system and are not copied here.

## Working Context

The syllabus ([[cancer-genomics-course-2026-syllabus]]) has four blocks: tumour evolution and phylogenies, liquid biopsy, spatial and single-cell methods, and molecular epidemiology. New this year were the liquid biopsy practical week ([[course-week-liquid-biopsy-2026-02-16]]), built on simulated cfDNA data so no patient data left governed storage, and a guest lecture by [[zoe-carmichael]] on reporting genomic results to clinicians.

Teaching assistants were [[kenji-mori]] and [[sofia-reyes-calderon]], 30 hours each; both asked to count it towards their graduate school teaching requirement, which was granted. [[maja-kovac]] taught the epidemiology block and [[amara-okonkwo]] the spatial practical. [[tobias-wren]] set up student accounts on Kestrel; the first-week login problems were the single most common complaint.

Final projects were presented on 2026-05-22 ([[course-final-project-presentations-2026-05-22]]). Six groups of four; the best project reproduced a published clonal deconvolution on public data and found a sample swap in the supplementary table.
