# Prediagnostic Detection Is Not Screening Evidence

## Stance

Finding a cancer signal in stored blood drawn before diagnosis shows that the signal exists before diagnosis. It does not show that a test would work as a screening test, that earlier detection would help anyone, or how much overdiagnosis it would cause. Our papers should say so plainly.

## Scope

All Northvale outputs, talks and press contacts, and the group's review writing on early detection.

## Why I Think This

- Cases in a cohort are clinically diagnosed cancers, so the design cannot see cancers a test would find that never surface ([[overdiagnosis]]).
- Detection rates in matched stored samples depend on threshold, storage and control selection, and published numbers differ several-fold ([[chen-2020-panseer-four-years-before-diagnosis]] against [[patel-2026-prediagnostic-methylation-cps3]]).
- A methylation marker has a long road from natural-history evidence to clinical use ([[pharo-2025-methylation-liquid-biopsy-roadmap]]).
- [[mei-lin-zhou]] holds the same view and enforces it under the collaboration agreement.

## What Would Change My Mind

Nothing about the logic; but a randomised screening trial using a similar marker would make our natural-history data more directly useful.

## Implications

No "predicts cancer" headlines. Talks show the roadmap and where Northvale stops. Any request from [[grace-tanaka]]'s communications office about the study gets this note first.
