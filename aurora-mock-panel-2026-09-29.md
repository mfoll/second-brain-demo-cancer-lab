# Aurora Mock Panel 2026-09-29

Internal mock review of the [[aurora-programme-grant-2027]] application, run on the Foundation's own scoring sheet two months before the deadline. Outcome: the programme moves from three aims to two, and the proteomics aim is merged into aim 2.

## At a Glance

> [!note]
> Decision: drop aim 3 as a separate aim and make plasma proteomics work package 2.3 inside the MRD aim, run on St Aldric post-surgery plasma only; Northvale aliquots stay reserved for cfDNA. Agreed by Ines and Marta at the end of the session, confirmed in writing on 2026-09-30.

## Who Was There

- Panel: [[leah-ostrander]] (chair, remote), [[jonas-eriksen]], [[margarethe-sell]].
- Applicants: Ines, [[marta-kowalczyk]].
- Observers: [[chloe-armitage]] (grants office, timekeeping), [[maja-kovac]] and [[dmitri-halvorsen]] (aim leads, listening only).

## Format

Panel members received [[aurora-aims-draft-v2]] and a ten-page case for support a week ahead. Ines presented for ten minutes, then 25 minutes of questions, as at the real Aurora interview. The panel scored privately on the Foundation's five criteria and then discussed openly. Written comments are kept in [[aurora-mock-panel-feedback]].

## What the Panel Said

- Jonas: aim 3 used the same Northvale and St Aldric plasma as aims 1 and 2 and had no hypothesis of its own; if aims 1 or 2 slipped, aim 3 had nothing to measure. He also pointed out that Northvale baseline aliquots are 0.5 mL and the biobank allows one withdrawal per case for this study ([[corvane-biobank]]), so cfDNA and proteomics would compete for the same tube.
- Margarethe: the budget read as three projects; the proteomics line had instrument time without a clear deliverable, which Aurora reviewers dislike.
- Leah, summing up: three aims made the programme read like three projects sharing a budget. The strongest story is one cfDNA feature set followed from years before diagnosis to relapse after surgery. Proteomics should serve that story, not sit beside it.
- On aim 1, the panel accepted the lead-time design but asked how reverse causation is handled; Maja's answer (the decision taken at the [[northvale-analysis-meeting-2026-06-24]]) satisfied them, though Leah asked that the excluded first year be stated in the aims page rather than buried in methods.
- On aim 2, the panel asked about external validation; Ines said this is the same issue as Reviewer 2 on the [[mrd-manuscript]] and is waiting on the data access policy.

Scores (out of 5, mean of three): significance 4.3, approach 3.3, feasibility 3.7, team 4.7, value for money 3.3. Approach and value for money were the weak criteria.

## Decision and Rationale

Marta proposed merging her aim into aim 2 before Ines did. In the post-surgery setting, plasma volume is not limiting (St Aldric draws 2 × 10 mL at each visit), and proteins can be tested as a second analyte for relapse detection against the same imaging endpoint. That gives proteomics a hypothesis of its own: whether a protein score adds to cfDNA for earlier relapse detection. Northvale proteomics is dropped. The question [[should-proteomics-be-an-aurora-work-package]] was answered the same day.

Options considered and rejected:

- Keep three aims and cut aim 3's budget. Rejected: reviewers would still see a dependent aim.
- Drop proteomics entirely. Rejected: the multi-analyte angle is what distinguishes this from our earlier single-study grants, and Rosa Lindgren had confirmed proteomics is in scope if it serves the cancer question.
- Move proteomics to aim 1 on Northvale samples. Rejected because of the aliquot conflict.
