# Open Peer Review Should Publish Reports, Not Names

## Stance

Journals should publish review reports and author responses by default, and leave reviewer names to the reviewer's choice.

## Scope

Applies to journals in our field, especially methods journals where reviewer critique of code and analysis is useful to readers. It does not cover grant review, where panels and confidentiality work differently.

## Why I Think This

- Published reports show readers what was checked and what was not. For a methods paper, a report saying "the code was run on the test data and reproduced the figures" is information.
- Reviewers get visible credit for work that currently disappears.
- Names are different. An early-career reviewer criticising a senior author's flagship method carries a real career risk. Opt-in signatures let senior reviewers sign, which normalises it, without forcing juniors to choose between candour and safety.
- At the [[editorial-board-meeting-2026-05-27]] nobody could point to evidence that mandatory signing improves report quality.

## What Would Change My Mind

- Data from the Genome Methods Letters trial showing that opt-in signing stays rare among senior reviewers, so the norm never shifts.
- Evidence that published unsigned reports are used to target reviewers anyway, by guessing identities.
- Evidence that signed reviews are measurably more constructive without lowering decline rates.

## Implications

- Ines supports the trial design and will draft reviewer guidance on that basis.
- In her own practice she is leaning towards signing ([[should-i-sign-my-peer-review-reports]]), which is consistent: the stance is about choice, not about secrecy.
- [[lena-vogt]] disagrees on the default; that is a live, friendly disagreement.
